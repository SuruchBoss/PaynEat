01 — Real shift conditions

A restaurant floor isn't a boardroom

Every design decision in this system was tested against four conditions that happen on every shift — not against a clean screen in a quiet office

01GlareA window table at midday — sun glare on the screen washes out contrast even further, and faint card borders disappear completely
02SteamThe kitchen screen sits somewhere nobody has time to wipe down — text has to be legible from three meters away
03Greasy handsOne mistap means one wrong dish. Kitchen buttons are sized large enough to hit accurately even with oily hands
04Flaky Wi-FiRestaurant Wi-Fi drops out from time to time. The system has to say so — not stay silent and quietly lose an order
02 — Kitchen screen

The ticket that's waited longest is always on top

Kitchen staff don't read tickets one by one — they scan for what needs doing next. Color does the sorting, not the decorating, and a ticket running late flags itself automatically, with nobody watching a clock

Over 15 minutes late → auto-flagged

Dine-in, takeaway, or delivery — obvious at a glance

Each ticket shows a different icon by order type, so you know instantly which one needs serving at a table and which one a customer is waiting to collect — without opening it. Takeaway or delivery orders start from a single button on the floor plan, with no table required, and takeaway orders get an automatic daily queue number

Dine-in · takeaway · delivery — distinct icons, same ticket

Buttons sized for a hand that's already full

The status buttons in the kitchen are nearly double the size of the same buttons on the phone app, because whoever's tapping is holding a pan in the other hand — hard to miss even with oily hands or gloves

Bigger buttons wherever hands are occupied — kitchen, tablet, cart

Still working when the Wi-Fi drops

If the live connection drops, the kitchen screen shows a full-width warning and starts polling for tickets every 30 seconds instead. The worst outcome is a screen that stays quiet while orders are piling up unseen

Falls back to polling every 30 seconds

03 — Readable

Legible no matter how bad the light is

The "pending" status used to be white text on amber — good-looking, but it disappeared under sun glare. That measured 2.13:1 — less than half of the 4.5:1 WCAG AA threshold. Text color is now picked per button based on its actual background, instead of white everywhere — legible in any light, with no squinting or tilting the screen away from the sun

Any staff role can switch this from their account page — it's not buried in an admin settings screen — and the choice sticks to the device, so a tablet sitting in direct sun can stay set that way no matter who's logged in

04 — Checkout

A receipt that always adds up

The payment line shows the actual cash a customer handed over, not just the amount applied to the bill — so the receipt always reconciles the way it should: cash given − change = bill total

Split bills, move tables, merge bills
Food cost, discounts, service charge and VAT are all split proportionally, automatically
Real printing via ESC/POS
Thermal printers on the same LAN/Wi-Fi network, 58mm and 80mm paper, full Thai character support
Abbreviated tax invoices
Sequential numbering by Buddhist calendar year, with a full history kept even when a wrong invoice is voided
Refunds that leave a trail
Full or partial, a reason is required every time, and it's automatically deducted from net sales
05 — Back office

The numbers an owner checks before closing up

Today's sales, an hour-by-hour chart, and the payment method breakdown all update live while you're still selling — no waiting for the shift to end to know how the day went

Shift open/close, cash count
Closing a shift automatically reconciles actual cash against the system total, and a shift must be open before any payment can be taken
Stock deducts itself
Link a dish to its ingredients and stock is deducted the moment it's sent to the kitchen — when an ingredient runs out, the dish is taken off sale automatically, no one has to remember
Promotions with real conditions
Time windows, specific dishes, minimum spend, discount codes, and buy-one-get-one — all matched automatically
5 staff roles
Enforced on the backend as well as on screen — not just a button hidden from view
Customers and loyalty points
Optionally attach a customer to an order while taking it, points accrue automatically based on spend, and their purchase history is available instantly
A tamper-proof activity trail
Every point where floor fraud typically happens is logged and can't be edited afterward — cancelled orders, discounts given, changed staff permissions — always with who did it and why

07 — AI assistant

Ask about sales in plain language, skip the report screens

Type a question in Thai or English and the AI assistant (powered by Claude) calls tools wired to the real restaurant database to pull the numbers — it never guesses from the model's general knowledge. Every answer always cites where its data came from.

Tools wired to real data only
Sales, best sellers, repeat customers, recent orders — every answer traces back to the exact endpoint it called
Says "no data" instead of guessing
It refuses to invent numbers for questions the system has no data for
Role-scoped access
Only the owner/manager can ask, with a daily question cap to keep API costs under control
