Real shift conditions

A restaurant floor isn't a boardroom

Every design decision in this system was tested against four conditions that happen on every shift — not against a clean screen in a quiet office

  1. 12:40window table

    Glare

    A window table at midday — sun on the screen washes out contrast even further, and faint card borders disappear completely

    → Text colour picked from the real background, never below 4.5:1

  2. 19:05kitchen screen

    Steam

    The kitchen screen sits somewhere nobody has time to wipe down — text has to be legible from three metres away

    → Big type, colour for priority, readable from 3 m

  3. 19:32at the wok

    Greasy hands

    One mistap means one wrong dish, and whoever taps is holding a wok in the other hand

    → Kitchen buttons nearly twice the size of the phone ones

  4. 20:10shop Wi-Fi

    Flaky Wi-Fi

    Restaurant Wi-Fi drops out from time to time. The system has to say so — not stay silent and quietly lose an order

    → Full-width warning, fallback fetch every 30 seconds

Kitchen screen

The ticket that's waited longest is always on top

Kitchen staff don't read tickets one by one — they scan for what needs doing next. Color does the sorting, not the decorating, and a ticket running late flags itself automatically, with nobody watching a clock

Over 15 minutes late → auto-flagged

Dine-in, takeaway, or delivery — obvious at a glance

Each ticket shows a different icon by order type, so you know instantly which one needs serving at a table and which one a customer is waiting to collect — without opening it. Takeaway or delivery orders start from a single button on the floor plan, with no table required, and takeaway orders get an automatic daily queue number

Dine-in · takeaway · delivery — distinct icons, same ticket

Buttons sized for a hand that's already full

The status buttons in the kitchen are nearly double the size of the same buttons on the phone app, because whoever's tapping is holding a pan in the other hand — hard to miss even with oily hands or gloves

Bigger buttons wherever hands are occupied — kitchen, tablet, cart

Still working when the Wi-Fi drops

If the live connection drops, the kitchen screen shows a full-width warning and starts polling for tickets every 30 seconds instead. The worst outcome is a screen that stays quiet while orders are piling up unseen

Falls back to polling every 30 seconds

Readable

Legible no matter how bad the light is

The "pending" status used to be white text on amber — good-looking, but it disappeared under sun glare. That measured 2.13:1 — less than half of the 4.5:1 WCAG AA threshold. Text color is now picked per button based on its actual background, instead of white everywhere — legible in any light, with no squinting or tilting the screen away from the sun

Any staff role can switch this from their account page — it's not buried in an admin settings screen — and the choice sticks to the device, so a tablet sitting in direct sun can stay set that way no matter who's logged in

Checkout

A receipt that always adds up

The payment line shows the actual cash a customer handed over, not just the amount applied to the bill — so the receipt always reconciles the way it should: cash given − change = bill total

Split bills, move tables, merge bills
Food cost, discounts, service charge and VAT are all split proportionally, automatically
Real printing via ESC/POS
Thermal printers on the same LAN/Wi-Fi network, 58mm and 80mm paper, full Thai character support
Abbreviated tax invoices
Sequential numbering by Buddhist calendar year, with a full history kept even when a wrong invoice is voided
Refunds that leave a trail
Full or partial, a reason is required every time, and it's automatically deducted from net sales
Back office

Meat counter · Wholesale

Sell meat by weight and bill wholesale customers, in one system

For restaurants with a fresh-meat or take-home counter, or that supply other shops on account. No second program to run.

  1. Weigh it, charge what it weighsPrice an item per kg, type the weight from the scale display and see the price before it goes in the cart — 0.485 kg × 1,200 = ฿582.00. Each bag is its own line and can be re-weighed before sending.
  2. Or scan the label, no typingAny USB or Bluetooth scanner works. An EAN-13 scale label gives the item and its weight in one scan; a misread label raises an alert instead of guessing the weight.
  3. Sell on credit, within a limitSet a credit limit and term per customer — a bill over the limit can’t be charged. See balances by age, issue billing notes, and take payments that settle the oldest bills first.
  4. The drawer still balancesCash brought in to settle a debt is counted in the drawer at close, and the Z-report lists debt payments separately from the day’s sales.

Security

A system that touches money has to be safe from day one

A POS holds both your sales and your customers' details. Security isn't a feature you bolt on later.

  • Passed an OWASP Top 10 reviewSeven findings, all fixed, every one of them fail-closed by default. No fallback secret baked into the code.
  • Permissions enforced on the server, not just hidden buttonsA hand-crafted request is refused too. A deactivated or re-roled account loses access immediately, without waiting for its token to expire.
  • An audit trail nobody can editVoided orders, discounts, refunds, menu price changes, staff role changes — who did it and why, every time.
  • Your data stays in your restaurantIt runs on a server or PC in your own shop. Your sales and customer data never go up to anyone else's server.
What to do before going liveThis project started as a portfolio piece, so some defaults aren't production-safe — and that isn't hidden. In production mode, the server refuses to start while the demo passwords are still in place. The full checklist is in SECURITY.md
